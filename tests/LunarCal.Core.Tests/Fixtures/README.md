# Lunar calendar fixtures (FR-LUNAR-3)

Reference data for `LunarCal.Core.Tests/Lunar`. Every value here comes from an external source. None is
generated by the engine under test, except the expected Vietnamese values in
`vn-hko-leap-differences.csv`, which are explained below.

## Sources

| --- | --- | --- |
| Spec §4.4 FR-LUNAR-3 | Tết 2023 (22/01), 2024 (10/02), 2025 (29/01), 2026 (17/02); leap month 2 in 2023 and 6 in 2025 | 2026-10-31 |

Hồ Ngọc Đức's own tables (`informatik.uni-leipzig.de/~duc/amlich/`) returned HTTP 404 on 2026-10-31, so the HKO
table is the main independent reference (ADR-0009).

## Files

- `hko-month-starts.csv`: `solar_date,lunar_year,lunar_month,is_leap`, the first day of every lunar month according to HKO.
- `hko-solar-terms.csv`: `solar_date,term`, the day each solar term starts according to HKO (`term` = `SolarTerm` member name).
- `vn-hko-leap-differences.csv`: the 13 HKO month starts where the Vietnamese leap month is different (see below).
- `parse_hko.py`: regenerates the two `hko-*.csv` files from the downloaded HKO text files:
  `python -I parse_hko.py <dir with T1901e.txt..T2100e.txt> <this dir>`. It uses only the Python standard library.

Known gap in the source: HKO's `T2069e.txt` has no row for 2069-12-30. That day is lunar day 17, so no month or
term boundary is lost.

## Why HKO and the Vietnamese calendar differ

HKO computes the Chinese calendar in UTC+8. The Vietnamese calendar uses UTC+7 (spec FR-LUNAR-1). The tests accept a
difference only when this one-hour offset explains it:

1. **Month start one day earlier in Vietnam** (77 of 2,474 month starts): the new moon falls between 16:00 and 17:00 UT.
   That is before midnight in UTC+7 and after midnight in UTC+8. The test computes the new moon instant and checks the window.
   Tết differs this way in 1903, 1935, 1965, 1968, 1969, 2007, 2030 and 2053. 1968 agrees with Wikipedia: North Vietnam,
   on UTC+7, celebrated on 29/01.
2. **Different leap month** (13 month starts in 1917, 1922, 1938, 1984–85 and 1987): the shifted day changes which month
   has no major solar term. `vn-hko-leap-differences.csv` lists the Vietnamese month that starts on each of these days.
   1984–85 matches Wikipedia. The other years are the engine's result, explained by the same mechanism but not checked
   against a second published Vietnamese table.
3. **Solar term one day earlier in Vietnam** (239 of 4,800): the sun reaches the term's longitude between 16:00 and 17:00 UT.
   The window is widened by 30 minutes for the precision of Hồ Ngọc Đức's sun-longitude series (≈0.01°). The largest
   observed deviation is 24 minutes (ADR-0007).

The counts are asserted exactly, so any change in the engine that moves even one date fails the tests.

## Note on dates before 1968

Calendars printed in Vietnam before 1968 (and in South Vietnam until 1975) were computed for UTC+8, as the Tết 1968
North/South split shows. The spec fixes UTC+7 for the whole range, so for those years this engine follows Hồ Ngọc Đức's
UTC+7 calculation and may differ from the calendars printed at the time (ADR-0006).
